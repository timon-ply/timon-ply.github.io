# Privacy at Wunschkiste

Last updated: 14 October 2026

## Controller

Timon Polley, Gutenbergstraße 5, 51469 Bergisch Gladbach, Germany. Email: [dev@timonply.com](mailto:dev@timonply.com). Further provider information is available in the [legal notice](/wunschkiste/impressum.en.html).

## Why Wunschkiste processes data

Wunschkiste helps collect and share gift ideas. Lists, wishes and reservations are stored on a server so invited people can view them in the app or browser. Using it without an account is therefore not exclusively local either.

For these features, Wunschkiste processes list titles, dates, descriptions, selected cover images, archive status, wishes with product addresses, image addresses, prices and notes, optional size and colour and up to five custom label/value details, reservations and their status. Optional uploaded cover and wish images are also stored. Only use images you are permitted to share; avoid confidential information, children's portraits or other unnecessary personal content.

The supported own image upload requires explicit confirmation of the [upload and use terms](/wunschkiste/terms.en/), version upload-v1-2026-10-11. A bounded receipt is stored with the current image, containing the version, the image's SHA-256 digest, permission scope, cover, wish or profile purpose and confirmation time. This receipt documents the limited permission and image association; it does not establish independently verified image rights. No unbounded upload-consent history is created. Replacing the image uses the current image's receipt; removing the image or confirming account erasure removes its association. Necessary report evidence and technical backups described below are separate. Processing provides the requested feature under Article 6(1)(b) GDPR and prevents abuse under Article 6(1)(f); parent permission is addressed below.

An optional account adds a display name, internal account identifier and technical device sessions to this data. New accounts use an email address and password through Google's Firebase Authentication. The app sends these details to Google over an encrypted connection; your password is not sent to the Wunschkiste backend. The backend receives and verifies authentication tokens and stores the account association, email address and verification status. Google also processes technical connection data such as IP addresses and information about the client used for authentication and abuse prevention. Further information is available in the [Firebase privacy notice](https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy).

If Google sign-in is offered, you can alternatively use your Google access. Google and Firebase Authentication process the account and authentication data needed for this; Wunschkiste receives verified proof of authentication, the account identifier, email address and, where available, display name. Your Google password is not sent to Wunschkiste. Signing in does not grant access to your inbox, contacts or files. Google's further processing is described in its [privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).

The iOS sign-in providers also receive technical connection data. [Google's iOS sign-in guidance](https://developers.google.com/identity/sign-in/ios/app-privacy) states that an IP address may help estimate a device's general location for fraud prevention. Wunschkiste requests no location permission for this and collects no GPS location. The privacy manifests of the included iOS libraries GoogleSignIn 10.0.0 and FirebaseMessaging 12.19.2 also declare device/user identifiers and technical usage, diagnostic and other data, partly for “Analytics” purposes. This is separate from including a Google Analytics SDK in Wunschkiste. With push enabled, FCM processes APNs tokens and installation identifiers in particular; [Google explains its iOS libraries' data processing](https://firebase.google.com/docs/ios/app-store-data-collection). Provider processing depends on the sign-in or delivery operation used; a library declaration does not mean every listed data type is collected in every use.

If Apple sign-in is configured and enabled in your app version, you can use your Apple access. Apple and Firebase Authentication process authentication; Wunschkiste receives the provider account identifier, email address or Apple's private relay address and, where provided, your shared name. Your Apple password is not sent to Wunschkiste. “Hide My Email” supplies a relay address, not your underlying real email address. Delivery and forwarding depend on Apple, Google and your settings; Wunschkiste does not guarantee delivery. Further information appears in [Apple's sign-in privacy notice](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/sign-in-with-apple/).

The new Apple/Google sign-in flow requires explicit, initially unchecked consent before each sign-in or renewed confirmation. With the same verified email address, Firebase may use existing email/Google access, associate trusted providers or replace a previously untrusted credential under its provider rules. This consent does not prevent such Firebase associations. Without consent, this new provider operation is not started; email sign-in and guest mode remain available alternatives where offered. [Google describes the provider rules](https://docs.cloud.google.com/identity-platform/docs/concepts-manage-users).

The prepared Apple browser-to-app return processes technical operation identifiers, verification proofs and necessary authentication/revocation tokens temporarily in the Firebase backend; sensitive handoff data is encrypted there. Email, name and provider identifiers may form part of the sign-in result. The public return address contains only a random operation identifier, never authentication tokens. The backend holds at most 128 Apple operations and 128 Google consent receipts, each valid for ten minutes. Google consent receipts contain technical verification hashes and account/provider associations, never provider or session tokens. Confirmed acknowledgment or cancellation removes held Apple token payloads. Expired technical records are cleaned up when subsequent operations start, so they are not necessarily physically deleted at the instant they expire. A limited provider-consent record is stored for a successfully associated account; further retention details appear below.

You can upload, replace or remove your own profile photo. It is stored as a private account image and retrieved only through authenticated account access. It does not automatically appear on public invitations or reservations. When removed, the selected photo can no longer be retrieved. A limited number of unused upload fragments may remain stored until a later upload after the technical protection period or until account deletion; they cannot be retrieved through the profile. Language and notification preferences are stored to implement your choices. Product names and other content you enter yourself are not sent to a translation service.

You can request a verification link or a link to reset your password. These messages are sent to your email address through Firebase Authentication. Successful email verification is required before the new access is associated with your Wunschkiste account. A stored device session and local authentication tokens enable continued use without entering your password every time you open the app. These credentials are confidential and are not displayed to other guests.

Older accounts initially retain their username and password sign-in. The Android app derives proof of authentication from the password on the device, and the backend processes it as a verifier with its own salt. Such proof remains confidential despite this processing. From your signed-in older account, you can set up verified email access; this additionally requires your previous password or, for accounts without a password, your previous account key to be checked. Lists and reservations remain associated with the same account. Older Wunschkiste accounts using usernames or account keys are not merged solely because names or email addresses match. The Firebase provider association described above is separate from this. After linking, email access replaces the previous authentication and recovery methods.

Older accounts may also have a private account key. This is an optional existing access method and is not required for new registration. Account keys, session permissions and guest permissions are credentials and do not belong in public messages.

Passkeys for the former app address do not apply to the new Firebase address. Passkey sign-in is disabled during migration; if offered again, the new address requires explicit new enrollment. Wunschkiste stores the public key, technical identifier, creation time and information used to securely verify authentication. Private keys and any biometric verification remain with the device or passkey provider; Wunschkiste receives no fingerprint or facial data. A synchronised passkey is also subject to your chosen provider's terms. If you configured a recovery code in an earlier version, the server stores it as a verifier. The Android app may keep a code encrypted until you confirm that it has been saved. Resetting an older account consumes the code and invalidates existing sessions, passkeys and old access credentials. This version does not offer passkey sign-in for email accounts.

Providing the requested list, account and gift features is based on Article 6(1)(b) GDPR. Technical measures against abuse and unauthorised access serve the legitimate interest in a secure service under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR. Required details are identified in the relevant form; the corresponding feature cannot be provided without required authentication or list data.

## Guest mode and data on your device

The first native iOS version uses Firebase email, Apple and Google sign-in where each provider is configured. The older username, account-key and passkey methods described above concern existing Android access; this iOS version does not offer them. An older account can be prepared for the new sign-in through the email-linking feature offered there.

Without an account, permission to edit your own lists or reservations is kept locally on your device or in your browser. This permission is separate from the public invitation link. Android protects local credentials with the Android Keystore; they are excluded from normal device backups. The web version uses local browser storage for necessary management and reservation permissions.

Storage serves the feature you requested and recognises your permission; it is not used for advertising or tracking across devices. If you clear app data or browser storage, lose your device or uninstall the app, guest access may be lost. Previously uploaded lists are not automatically deleted from the server as a result. Save any management access you need, or link your lists beforehand through the available account feature.

Whether you have seen or skipped the optional introduction is stored locally, separately from your credentials. This setting remains after you sign out. The introduction can be reopened in Help; your learning progress is not analysed.

On iOS, Wunschkiste stores session and Firebase authentication tokens, guest permissions, unconfirmed drafts and the proofs needed for sign-in and deletion recovery in the device-bound Apple Keychain. These entries use protection after the first unlock of this device; the app does not configure them for synchronisation to other devices. Language and introduction status are stored separately in local app preferences. Signing out removes account access; local guest permissions may remain. Uninstalling is neither account deletion nor a reliable promise that all Keychain data is removed. [Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/keychain-data-protection-secb0694df1a/web) describes the protection technology.

For custom cover, wish or profile photos, iOS uses the system photo picker. Wunschkiste processes the selected image rather than uploading your entire photo library. Before upload, it is rendered as a new JPEG with limited dimensions and file size. An unfinished box or wish draft, or a pending image upload, may retain the selected image locally for recovery. With content review enabled, guests see a preset cover until the exact uploaded version is approved; its authorized creator can preview the pending image. Profile photos remain within authenticated account access. Own wish photos are also replaced by neutral visuals for guests until their exact image digest and stored image revision are approved; a replacement requires new review.

## Invitations, reservations and product images

Anyone who knows a valid invitation link or code can view the corresponding available box and its contents, including description and date. Share this access information only with intended people. With content review enabled and in the new iOS/web interface, gift-givers’ names remain hidden. Older Android versions and boxes without content review may offer explicit name sharing; only the signed-in guest’s own agreement can make their display name visible on reservations and related notifications there. Email addresses and profile photos are not published. Technical associations still allow changing your own reservations; this is not complete anonymity towards the service provider.

The “Hide reservations from me” setting hides details about reserved or purchased wishes from the creator in the signed-in app and related notifications. It is a display preference and does not provide secrecy from people who can open the public guest view. Guests still see wish availability.

Product details are entered by you. The prepared link helper may derive a possible name solely from the path of an HTTPS link you provide. It does not fetch a retailer page or import a retailer photo, price or description. The suggestion may be wrong and needs your review. Without an approved own wish image, neutral gift visuals are used. The wish address and details you enter are stored to provide the box.

Opening a shop link calls the retailer externally, under its own privacy notices. Older Android versions may still display external image addresses of previously saved wishes, giving the image provider connection data, particularly your IP address. A price you entered is not a current shop quote. A click or reservation does not trigger a purchase in Wunschkiste. Any later automatic importer will require a separate review of merchant permission and its data processing.

## Notifications

Push notifications about new guests or purchased wishes are off by default. You can enable and disable them individually. Independently of these switches, a limited activity overview of the latest, up to 20 events is stored in the account. It comes from actual actions in your lists, remains available with push switched off and is not used for analytics or advertising.

Push messages are only offered if delivery is configured. After you enable them, the app may additionally request your device's notification permission. To send through Firebase Cloud Messaging, technical device identifiers and delivery tokens are associated with your signed-in device; at most ten device sessions are considered. Messages contain no gift-givers' names or profile images and no private credentials. Google processes the data required for delivery. The connection is encrypted; Firebase Cloud Messaging itself does not provide end-to-end encryption. See the [Firebase privacy notice](https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy) and [FCM message security guidance](https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/encryption).

Turning off push messages or device permission does not automatically remove notifications already stored in the account. After signing out or revoking a device session, that session is no longer used for delivery; deleting the account removes stored notifications, settings and device associations. Voluntary name sharing and push activation are based on your consent under Article 6(1)(a) GDPR; you can withdraw it for the future.

On iOS, delivery uses Firebase Cloud Messaging and Apple Push Notification service (APNs). Google and Apple process installation/delivery identifiers and technical delivery data. iOS banners use the title “Wunschkiste” and a generic message about a new guest or purchased wish; they contain no item, box or person names. The technical payload contains public event and box identifiers that do not grant editing permission. The app initialises FCM only for enabled, signed-in use with device permission. Previously confirmed activation can be restored on a later launch without prompting for permission again.

Generic messages already handed to the delivery service may still be displayed by the operating system after sign-out, disabling notifications or session revocation. iOS delivery requests a short five-minute expiry; this guarantees neither delivery nor immediate recall of accepted messages. Signing out or disabling notifications removes the Wunschkiste device association for future delivery and attempts to remove the local FCM token and delivered device notifications. The service and operating system may still retain identifiers or processed messages under their own rules. See the [FCM guidance for Apple platforms](https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/ios/get-started).

## Retention and deletion

After a Premium purchase, account deletion retains a private anti-reuse record: a SHA-256 digest of the store, environment and original purchase identifier, the deleted internal account identifier, a provider timestamp and, where applicable, the identifier of a revoked transaction. It contains no name, email address, raw Google purchase token or random purchase-account identifier. It cannot sign in to or restore the deleted account. The current flow has no automatic expiry for this record, so deletion after a fixed further period is not promised. Contact the controller with questions about its continued necessity or erasure.

Accounts, lists and wishes are retained to provide the ongoing service until deleted through the available features or following a justified request. Archiving hides a list from the active overview and does not delete data; invitations and reservations remain accessible. Removing individual wishes may initially allow restoration. Uninstalling the app or clearing local data does not replace server deletion.

The new provider sign-in flow stores the latest limited consent record in the account, including the explanation version, provider, consent time and acknowledgment time. An additional bounded proof per device session supports safe retry after a lost server response and includes technical verification hashes. No unbounded consent history is created. The account record remains until replaced by a newer record or account deletion. The session proof remains until replaced, cleanup of the relevant session or account deletion. They are separate from the technical operations described above that are usable for only ten minutes.

Deleting an account removes its associated lists, wishes, uploaded cover, wish and profile photos, device sessions, delivery associations, settings, stored notifications and invitations; its reservations in other people's lists are released. For Firebase accounts, deletion of the associated Firebase Authentication account is also initiated. An Apple-associated account requires fresh Apple confirmation for the same account and confirmation of provider revocation. Your general Google or Apple Account is not deleted. Other people's independent lists are retained. Deletion may require several steps; the app confirms completion only after successful server confirmation. Minimal technical deletion and blocking identifiers may remain to prevent old credentials from being reused. Details and a contact method outside the app are available under [Delete account and data](/wunschkiste/deletion.en.html).

After confirmed image removal, the private image store may retain empty objects with zero image bytes and bounded technical deletion fences. They prevent a delayed upload from restoring a removed image under the same object identifier. These records may include internal owner, list/account, resource and object identifiers and bounded status and capacity data; the fence contains no image content, names or email addresses and cannot sign in to or restore a deleted account. Historically occupied object slots are not released by removing an image. These technical fences currently have no automatic expiry, so complete removal of every technical reference or later release of all capacity is not promised. Contact the controller with questions about their continued necessity or erasure.

Google states that Firebase Authentication retains logged IP addresses for a few weeks. Other authentication data is kept until user deletion is requested and then removed from live and backup systems within 180 days. For Firebase Cloud Messaging, Google retains installation identifiers until an explicit identifier-deletion request; a provider period of up to 180 days then also applies. Removing the Wunschkiste device association is separate from deleting this identifier. The app's completion confirmation does not promise immediate removal of all provider identifiers or backup copies. Source: [Firebase privacy notice](https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy).

Security data and support messages are retained only while necessary to handle requests, protect the service or fulfil legal obligations. Technical backups may contain deleted data until they are overwritten normally. You can ask the controller for specific information about retention of your data.

Guest use in the new supported flows requires an explicit adult declaration; this is not proof of age. Public invitation links can be forwarded and do not independently verify a viewer's age. Share only necessary information, without a child's full name, address, birth year or photo. If a child creates a Firebase identity after approval but abandons verification or the request expires, expiry does not automatically erase that identity. ## Reports, blocks and content review

The safety features prepared for the iOS test version use the existing Firestore backend. A report contains a reason, optional description, the affected box or wish identifier and a bounded content excerpt. Technical request identifiers support retrying the same report after connection problems. Raw passwords, authentication tokens and private invitation keys are not included in reports. Blocks are associated with your account or a server-hashed guest capability; other users do not receive the reporter’s identity. Management may show the already known box title if it remains allowed.

A word and link filter limits objectionable content but does not replace complete review. Guests continue to see a preset cover until an operator approves the exact uploaded cover version. Replacements require new approval. Uploading therefore does not mean a photo is already publicly visible. Blocking applies to the particular access identity; anonymous guest capabilities are not verified person identities and can be replaced. Previously shared copies cannot be recalled.

The queue and receipts are bounded. New requests are rejected at capacity; capacity is not automatically expanded. Closed reports are intended for manual cleanup after 90 days; open reports remain until handled. There is no automatic deletion job. Own blocks are removed upon confirmed account deletion, and a bounded pseudonymous reporter reference remains in received reports after account deletion until handling or manual cleanup; it cannot be used to sign in. Necessary evidence about another user’s reported content may remain to handle reports and defend legitimate claims. The legal bases are providing the safety functions and legitimate interests in preventing abuse under Article 6(1)(b) or (f) GDPR.

The browser retains at most one pending report draft with a hashed access association and bounded description in the current tab. iOS retains a bounded number of pending drafts in the device Keychain for exact retries. A report is not shown as delivered before the backend confirms receipt. Confirmed account deletion removes local iOS drafts associated with that account. Activation and the actual operator response process must be checked before public availability.
